## 🧬 Research

### Cellular responses to genetic perturbation

I use large-scale single-nucleus transcriptomics to investigate how genetic perturbations reshape cellular states across biological contexts.

My doctoral work included analysis of **>100,000 nuclei** across physiological and disease conditions to characterize neuronal and glial transcriptional programs, changes in cellular composition, and multicellular communication following tau ablation.

### Spatial organization of disease

I integrate **spatial transcriptomics, single-nucleus RNA-seq, and quantitative imaging** to understand how molecular programs vary across tissue microenvironments.

My work in Alzheimer's disease and cerebral amyloid angiopathy identified distinct transcriptional responses associated with vascular and parenchymal amyloid environments.

### Toward predictive models of cellular state

I am increasingly interested in computational approaches that move beyond describing biological states toward **learning how cellular systems respond to perturbation**.

Current interests include probabilistic modeling, representation learning, multimodal integration, and generative approaches for modeling cellular state transitions and perturbation responses.

## 📂 Featured Project

### ✨ multiomic-spatial-integration

A computational framework for integrating **single-nucleus RNA-seq with spatial transcriptomics** to infer cell-type-specific programs and study how cellular states vary across tissue microenvironments.

The repository includes reproducible workflows for:

- Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic integration
- Cell-type signature learning
- Spatial cell-type deconvolution
- Statistical modeling across biological conditions
- Cell-state and microenvironment analysis
- Reproducible analysis in Python/R and HPC environments

This project reflects my broader interest in developing computational approaches that connect **cellular identity, spatial context, and biological state** from multimodal measurements.
